# Generation 1

Inspired by Tony Fadell's *Build* (three generations; first adventure). Encode the method, not the book. Do not quote the book.

## Goal

Define **Generation 1**: small enough to ship, complete enough that the story is true. If you only shipped this, a customer should still feel the why.

This is not a disposable "MVP." A first iPod still had to be a thousand songs in your pocket. Broken promise + "we'll fix it in V2" is not Gen 1.

**Three generations (for context, do not build all three now):**

1. Find whether the story is real (this skill).
2. Fix it and take it to more people (marketing is part of this, not an afterthought).
3. Make the business hold.

Gen 1 is guided by **vision, then customer insight, then data**, not the reverse.

## Before you start

1. Read `templates/version-zero.md`.
2. Need a sayable why. If missing, run `product-story` (or extract four lines now).
3. Read `ideas/<slug>.md` if it exists.

## Interview

1. **Promise**: The why in one line. Gen 1 must keep it.
2. **Press release**: Write it as if announcing Gen 1 *today*. If it needs a paragraph of roadmap, Gen 1 is too big or the promise is fuzzy.
3. **First experience**: Discover → first use. What they see, do, feel.
4. **Must stay true**: 1–3 moments; if they fail, the story is a lie.
5. **Handcuffs**: What will not change so the rest can. Time, team, platforms.
6. **Not this generation**: Vision, not this ship. Be explicit.
7. **Heartbeat**: Cadence in weeks/months, not hour estimates.

## Cut the scope

| In Gen 1 | Later (Gen 2+) | Never (for this product) |
|---|---|---|
| Required to keep the promise | Real, not this ship | Contradicts the why |

Reject: platform/admin before the customer moment; "while we're here"; personalization, notifications, dashboards unless they *are* the promise.
