# Product story

Inspired by Tony Fadell's *Build* (why storytelling). Encode the method, not the book. Do not quote the book.

## Goal

A story a person who does not work here could repeat. **Why before what.** Features are not the story. If it needs a slide deck, it is not ready.

A good product story: speaks to head *and* heart; makes the complicated simple; reminds people of the problem (the why).

## Before you start

1. Read `templates/story-card.md`.
2. Read `ideas/<slug>.md` if it exists.
3. Ask; do not invent the customer or the stakes.

## Interview

Ask until answers are concrete.

1. **Person**: Who, in one life moment (not a segment label).
2. **Why exist**: Why will they need it, buy it, love it?
3. **Head and heart**: What they think is broken; what they fear or hope.
4. **Simple**: One line as clear as a device promise, not a feature list. Analogy only if it clarifies.
5. **Not this**: What people will assume you are, that you are not.
6. **Press release**: Headline + first paragraph as if Gen 1 shipped. Marketing starts here, not after build.

## Write the story

Four lines, no jargon:

1. **Who + moment**
2. **Problem as they feel it**
3. **What changes**
4. **Why we are the ones**

Then: would someone who does not work here care in 15 seconds? Score 1–5 and say why.

Also write the same truth for:

- A smart friend
- An exec (outcome, risk, ask)
- An engineer (the job and the hard constraint)

If those three contradict, the story is not done.

## Save

Update `ideas/<slug>.md` **Story**, and write `ideas/<slug>-story.md` from the story-card template.

If they cannot get to a sayable why, they are not ready to build. Point back to `worth-making`.

If the why is sayable, offer `marketing-method` so the story has a home at each step, not only in a pitch.
