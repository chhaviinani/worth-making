# worth-making

A small Cursor kit, inspired by Tony Fadell's *Build: An Unorthodox Guide to Making Things Worth Making*, for sitting with an idea and asking better questions.

You describe what you are making. The agent walks you through the same kinds of questions Tony Fadell would: why it exists, who it is for, what Generation 1 really is, and when you would stop. You leave with a brief you can actually share.

If you do not know something yet, that is fine. It stays `unknown`. No invented customers, no fake traction, no why you did not earn.

If *Build* already lives in your head, this is a way to keep it next to the work. The stories stay in the book. The decisions happen here, with you.

## Get started

1. Open this folder in Cursor.
2. In chat, type:

   ```
   run worth-making
   ```
3. Tell it the idea in a few sentences. Answer as you go. Treat it like a kind studio crit, not an exam.

Product briefs land in `ideas/`. Career, hiring, CEO, and board briefs land in `career/`.

PMs, founders, designers, engineers, managers: you are all welcome. If you make things, this is for you.

## What you can do

- Check whether an idea is worth making now
- Write a story someone outside the team can repeat
- Map every touchpoint, not only the product
- Define Generation 1 so it still keeps the promise
- Tell a data call from a vision call
- Prep a hire, a hard conversation, or a board page

When you finish one skill, it will nudge you toward the next (`product-story`, `version-zero`, `mentor-map`, and friends).

The skills follow the six parts of *Build*. They are questions and templates, not copied chapters.

## Principles

Straight from the book, kept close:

1. Why before what.
2. One customer. Not consumer and enterprise at the same time.
3. Painkiller, not vitamin.
4. Generation 1 keeps the promise.
5. Name the decision. Data cannot solve a vision problem. Vision cannot hide from data that exists.
6. Prototype the journey, not only the object.
7. Constraints are the design.
8. Same truth in every room.
9. Kind and clear with people.

## About

Tony Fadell's work, and *Build* especially, has helped me a lot on my PM journey (and continues to!!). I made this repo for myself, and for anyone else who wants the book close at hand when they have an idea and need more understanding: is it worth making, who is it for, what ships first, what constraints must hold, and so on.
